# Should students still take exams in SWPP now that AI capabilities have advanced beyond what they were when the course was designed?

I like to address three points about this situation.

I don't have huge ambitions about my grade - I just want to graduate. But since this is SNU anyway, I can sense there are lots of collegues who are intereseted in getting good grade, and preparing for their best to do as best as they can whenever they face any assignment. I think that is a good thing. To some degree, it is better for students to be motivated with a hope of better grade. What makes me concerned is that this kind of swift change of course design gives huge uncertainty on many students' study schedules and planning, and I see it discourages some, and sometimes ambicious students. But I also understand that as an educator, it can be hard to predict and adjust to the breathtaking pace of recent AI advancement in development tools. I think it is sensible for many people to regret about not having clear sense of how this class was going to work in the first place.

Secondly, I believe that if the test should be held (unfortunatly my body do not support this as it wants more rest and test provides nervousness from uncertainty and variance deriving from daily conditions. haha. it is sad to be finite being) I think it can be best to focus on compiler optimization stratages, rather than focusing on nurturing good development customs. Partly it is because, even though Claude Code has ability to manage huge amount of specification, it is still a hard work for any team to have a clear understanding of the project specs. The fact that there exists already about 40 discussions proves that many teams are trying to make clear what is the best meaning of documentations. I believe those kinds of discussion somehow (if not always in a good way) helps getting better knowledge in development rules and tradictions. I always sense many discussion questions are sharp and sensible. And even though Claude Code does help making github CI code, it still cannot replace students in approving every PRs with sensible code reviews and documenting every sprints. So, in terms of Github stratege, I think Claude Code is not perfect, considering that project policy is regularly "clarified" and human developers have to adjust to that in regular terms.

I see TAs carefully replying about project specs at 23 PM April 24th - they are doing hard works. I see passion here from both students and TAs. It is a sad zetigist for us to do this kinds of discussion. Because in old good days, all of this should have been worked as a positive aspects. Now they aren't enough, and sometimes unproductive. And at the beginning this course was not ready for that change.

I am optimistic about the future of Korean CSE, if not for a good reason. It is going to be AI versus AI, not AI versus human. The claim from the professor that we need better education in era of Claude Mythos is basically right. The time required for any software vulnerability to be translated to explotiation is decreasing rapidly. In 2018, it took on average 2.3 years to exploit any known software vulnerability. Now it takes only 2 days. We should be preapred for more speedy vuluerability checks, more speedy software updates, because the malicious hackers are now moving in a speed of light with the help of "state of art" LLM models. It is already hurting real business; last year cyber hacking in M&S and Jagure factory lines costed hugely. In case of Jauar Land Rover cyberattack, the anonymous hacker managed to halt all car factory's production line, and it took the total damage to the British economy of £1.9 billion. Now Mythos is finding 27 years old bug from FreeBSD operating system, and maybe sooner or later Chinese open source model may catch up within few years, if not months. Malicious actors will include not just individuals, but state actors like China, North Korea, or Iran. It is their interest to hack important finantial infrastructures or popular mobiel platforms. All software companies should be prepared for more rapid, regular kinds of software patches, especially for small companies who are not afford to use best Claude Code services. Expect many Github open source project to be in crisis as they don't have financial clout of American big tech companies. And many South Korean IT companies, including its government, can be in such a position in the near future, as we don't have GPUs as many as America's. But teaching students how to catch up AI speed while maintaining junior expert level of accuracy is going to be extremely challenging.

These are interesting days to live as a CSE major. We are already in a tricky situation, and I see that tradeoff is going to be made. I hope we can get best decisions for this course, maybe for our future generations. It should be an electrifying class in a good way.
