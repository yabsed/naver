# What I wrote about Iran in January 2026.

*2026-01-18*

Why do people have to go through this terrible cycle every few years? Does not make sense. South Korea is a country of revolution. We know how to fight. But at least in the past, we had reasons to be hopeful, to be cheerful. What is happening in Iran is just wrong. People heroically fight, but the government has already killed all the viable opposition. How should a man deal with a system which steals every reason to be hopeful, leaving only with despair? It hurts more. It hurts.

---

*2026-01-18*

The true tragedy of the Iranian regime lies not merely in its incompetence, but in the heartbreaking scale of its squandered potential. While nations like China and South Korea soared by embracing technology and merit, Iran’s immense promise was allowed to wither in the shadow of dogma and corruption. By prioritizing blind loyalty over expertise, the regime did not just fail the economy; they suffocated the nation's future, driving a massive exodus of its brightest minds and turning a land of rich history into a monument of lost opportunity.

​Yet, strictly measuring the economy feels irrelevant now. What remains is tragedy and horror. We know the regime will not hesitate to arrest or kill; recent history is written in blood. The grim precedent of 2022 serves as a haunting reminder regarding the price of dissent. During that crackdown, hundreds of protesters lost their lives and countless others were imprisoned, with the regime going as far as handing down death sentences to silence opposition. The people on the streets know this better than anyone. The government's decision to sever the internet was not merely a disruption of daily life; it was an act to blind the eyes of those who were desperately studying how to neutralize tear gas and treat gunshot wounds.

​They seek freedom and dignity knowing the cost is death. For years, I have witnessed nothing but the shattering of Iran’s hope on the streets. We cannot let such blazing courage be extinguished in the dark again. This cannot be another chapter of tragedy. The world must keep its eyes open, for their cry for freedom deserves to be heard, not silenced by a tyranny devoid of soul.

*2026-01-18*

Iran, having been cut off from the internet for several days, has become a giant black box. No one knows whether hundreds or thousands are dying each day. The intensive care units in Tehran and Shiraz are paralyzed, overwhelmed by patients and corpses struck by military gunfire aimed precisely at their heads and chests. But I fear that the deaths of citizens are merely a necessary condition for revolution, not a sufficient one.

For the past half-day, I have been running painful simulations in my head regarding the potential success of this revolution. Just as YS (Kim Young-sam) did in Korea, could opposition forces like Pahlavi manage to negotiate with that detestable military faction and ultimately topple the regime? If the US and Israel intervened—a surgical strike, a decapitation—could it shatter the Islamic regime and IRGC? If Starlink terminals were successfully smuggled in, could they pierce through Iran's internet blackout?

In the midst of my searching, I suddenly wondered: What is the point of all this?

*2026-02-19*

What I see from Iran now is neither an Islamic Republic nor the Persian Empire. The protest became completely meaningless. And with that, this country has become something completely meaningless, too. It is a terrifying void. In the midst of this reality, everyone is left with only three grim choices: to die, to become an aggressor, or to bear witness. In the short term, there was simply nothing else anyone could do—not even Donald Trump. This is not a democracy game anymore. The best understanding of Iran right now is that it is a giant Squid Game machine.

*2026-02-24*

Everyone who speaks Farsi has now become mentally ill. A person's body can stay alive even after their spirit has died. I am scared. I cannot feel their pain. It can never be cured.

In the West, goodness that fails to triumph is no goodness at all. When noble values are at stake, watching helplessly due to a lack of power does not absolve you of guilt. It becomes the grave sin of incompetence.
