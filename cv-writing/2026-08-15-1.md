I never expected to find Jane Street on The Economist's list of fools. What's striking about the July loss is that the month was a favorable environment for market makers. If they lost money, it wasn't in market making. This shows that many quant trading firms, as they scale up, agonize over what to do with their excess capital. Competitor HRT's strategic operation of a hedge-fund-style unit like Prism is an extension of the same problem. This episode suggests that Jane Street's business model may be more volatile than previously assumed.

Jane Street is still a company that makes money very well, and even with its current investment approach, the mood could reverse in the short term if Jane Street scores a big win in the course of Anthropic's listing. However, it is questionable whether that constitutes a sustainable form of prop trading. There are likely more universes in which Jane Street succeeds than ones in which it fails. The LinkedIn profiles of its employees are probably safe — given the talent I've seen in them firsthand. But we now know just how deep the failure universes run. The price of that reassessment: 311bps on the new bonds against 152 on the retired ones — and note who paid it. A reassessment Jane Street bought with its own money.
